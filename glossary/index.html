Finance and Compliance Glossary

Plain-language definitions of the terms banks, investors and authorities use. Definitions are general information, and rules change, so confirm the current position before you act.

ABCDEFGHILMNOPRSTUVW

A

AOA (Articles of Association)
The document that sets the internal rules of a company, such as how directors are appointed, how meetings are held and how shares are transferred. It is filed with the Registrar at incorporation and read together with the Memorandum of Association. Read more
AOC-4
The form in which a company files its financial statements with the Registrar of Companies after the annual general meeting. It must be filed within the time the law prescribes, and late filing attracts additional fees for every day of delay. Read more
Audited financials
Financial statements examined by an independent auditor who gives an opinion on whether they present a true and fair view. Banks, investors and tender authorities usually prefer audited accounts, and many entities are legally required to have them. Read more

B

Bank guarantee
A promise by a bank to pay a beneficiary if the customer fails to meet an obligation. Contractors use it as bid security or performance security in tenders, and the bank charges a fee and may ask for margin money. Read more
Bid security
A deposit or guarantee that a bidder gives with a tender to show it is serious. It is returned to unsuccessful bidders and may be forfeited if the winner backs out. It is also called earnest money deposit. Read more
Book value
The value of a company's assets less its liabilities as shown in its books. It is a starting point for net asset valuation, but it may differ from market value because of intangible assets and the age of assets. Read more
Break-even point
The level of sales at which total revenue equals total cost, so there is neither profit nor loss. Banks and investors look at it in projections to see how much the business must sell before it starts earning. Read more

C

Cap table
A table showing who owns what in a company: each shareholder, the shares they hold, the percentage and the class of shares. Investors read it to understand dilution, and it must match the company's statutory records. Read more
Capex (capital expenditure)
Money spent on long-term assets such as machinery, buildings or software that will be used for more than a year. Capex is not expensed at once; it is spent now and depreciated over its useful life. Read more
Cash credit (CC)
A working capital facility in which a bank lets a business draw up to a sanctioned limit against its stock and receivables, and pay interest only on the amount used. The limit is reviewed periodically. Read more
CIN
The Corporate Identification Number, a unique 21-character code the Registrar of Companies gives to each company at incorporation. It appears on the incorporation certificate and on official documents of the company. Read more
CMA report
A set of financial forms in a bank's format that presents past results, the current estimate and projections so the bank can assess a loan request. It covers operating statement, balance sheet analysis, ratios, MPBF and fund flow. Read more
Comparable company analysis
A valuation method that values a business using the multiples at which similar companies trade or are sold, such as revenue or EBITDA multiples. It depends on choosing good peers and adjusting for size and risk. Read more
Contribution margin
Revenue from a unit or order less the variable costs of making and delivering it. It shows how much each sale contributes towards fixed costs and profit, and is central to unit economics in consumer businesses. Read more
Current ratio
Current assets divided by current liabilities. It shows whether a business can meet its short-term obligations. Banks often look for a ratio above about 1.2 to 1.33, though norms vary by bank and industry. Read more

D

DCF (discounted cash flow)
A valuation method that estimates the value of a business as the present value of its expected future cash flows, discounted at a rate that reflects risk. It includes a terminal value for years beyond the forecast. Read more
Debt-to-equity ratio
Total debt divided by shareholders' equity. A higher ratio means more of the funding is borrowed, which raises risk for lenders. Banks often track a similar ratio called total outside liabilities to tangible net worth. Read more
Depreciation
The spreading of the cost of a long-term asset over its useful life. It reduces profit in the books but is not a cash payment, which is why it is added back when calculating cash flow and DSCR. Read more
Designated partner
A partner in an LLP who has extra responsibilities for compliance and filings. An LLP needs at least two designated partners, and at least one must be a resident in India. Read more
DIN
The Director Identification Number, a unique number allotted to a person who is or wants to become a director of a company. It stays with the person for life and is needed for incorporation filings. Read more
Discount rate
The rate used to convert future cash flows into present value. It reflects the return investors expect for the risk of the business. A small change in the discount rate can change a valuation a lot. Read more
DPIIT
The Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade, the government department that recognises eligible entities as startups under the Startup India initiative and administers related schemes. Read more
DSC (digital signature certificate)
An electronic credential used to sign documents and file forms online, issued by a licensed certifying authority. Directors need it for company filings, and bidders need it to sign e-tenders. Read more
DSCR
The debt service coverage ratio: cash available for repayment divided by the instalment and interest due in a year. Many lenders look for an average of roughly 1.25 to 1.5 or higher, though each bank sets its own norm. Read more

E

E-tender
A tender floated and submitted online through a government or private portal. Bidders register, sign with a digital signature and upload technical and financial bids before the deadline. Read more
EBITDA
Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation. It approximates the cash profit from operations before financing and accounting charges, and is widely used in valuation multiples and loan assessment. Read more
EMD (earnest money deposit)
A deposit a bidder pays with a tender to show it is serious. It is returned to unsuccessful bidders. Micro and small enterprises may be exempt in some central procurement, depending on the tendering body. Read more
Enterprise value
The value of a business's operations, before taking its debt and cash into account. Equity value is found by subtracting net debt from enterprise value. Read more
EPR registration
Extended Producer Responsibility registration, which requires producers, importers and brand owners of certain products, such as plastic packaging and electronics, to take responsibility for end-of-life collection and recycling under environmental rules. Read more
Equity value
The value that belongs to shareholders: enterprise value less net debt. It is the figure used to work out the value per share in a valuation. Read more
ESOP
An employee stock option plan, which gives employees the right to buy company shares at a set price in future. Only companies can grant ESOPs, not LLPs, and the plan must follow company law. Read more

F

Free cash flow
The cash a business generates after operating costs, tax and the investment needed to maintain and grow it. It is the basis of DCF valuation, because it is what could be paid to investors. Read more
Fund flow statement
A statement showing where a business gets funds from, such as profit and borrowings, and where it uses them, such as capital spending and loan repayment. Banks read it to see whether long-term uses are met from long-term sources. Read more

G

GeM
The Government e-Marketplace, an online platform through which government departments and public bodies buy goods and services. Sellers register to receive orders and bid for tenders. Read more
Gross margin
Revenue less the direct cost of goods or services sold, shown as a percentage of revenue. It indicates how much is left to cover overheads and profit, and banks watch its stability across years. Read more
GSTIN
The Goods and Services Tax Identification Number, a 15-character number allotted to each GST registration. It appears on tax invoices and is used for filing returns and claiming input tax credit. Read more

H

HSN code
A standard code that classifies goods for tax purposes. The Harmonised System of Nomenclature code is shown on GST invoices, and the number of digits required depends on the turnover of the business. Read more

I

IEC
The Importer Exporter Code, a registration issued by the Directorate General of Foreign Trade that most importers and exporters need to carry out foreign trade. Read more
Input tax credit (ITC)
The credit a registered business can claim for GST paid on purchases, which is set against the GST it collects on sales. Claims depend on valid invoices, filing returns and meeting the conditions in the law. Read more
Interest coverage ratio
Operating profit divided by interest expense. It shows how many times a business can pay its interest from profit. Lenders prefer a comfortable margin above one. Read more
ISO certification
Confirmation by an accredited body that an organisation's management system meets an international standard, such as ISO 9001 for quality. Some buyers and tenders ask for it as proof of consistent processes. Read more

L

LLP
A Limited Liability Partnership, a legal entity that combines partnership flexibility with limited liability for partners. It cannot issue shares and has lighter annual compliance than a company. Read more
LMPC
The Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) registration, required for packers, importers and manufacturers of many pre-packaged goods. It covers declarations on the label, such as net quantity and maximum retail price. Read more

M

Margin money
The part of a project cost or a limit that the borrower funds from its own resources. In bank guarantees, it is the cash a bank holds as security against the guarantee. Read more
MGT-7
The form in which a company files its annual return with the Registrar of Companies. It gives details of shareholders, directors, meetings and other particulars as at the end of the year. Read more
MOA (Memorandum of Association)
The founding document of a company that states its name, registered office, objects and capital. Actions outside the objects can be challenged, so the objects clause should be drafted with the business in mind. Read more
Moratorium
A period at the start of a loan during which no principal is repaid, often to allow a project to start earning. Interest may still be payable. A moratorium lowers early repayment pressure and affects DSCR in later years. Read more
MPBF
Maximum permissible bank finance: the most a bank may lend for working capital under a given method. Under the second method of lending, it is 75% of current assets less other current liabilities. Read more
MSME
Micro, small and medium enterprises, classified by investment and turnover under the MSME law. Registered units may receive benefits such as priority lending, procurement preferences and payment protections. Read more

N

Net asset value
The value of a business based on its assets less its liabilities. It suits asset-heavy or loss-making companies and often sets a minimum value in a valuation. Read more
Net worth
Shareholders' funds: share capital plus reserves, less accumulated losses. Tenders and banks use net worth to judge financial strength. Tangible net worth excludes intangible assets. Read more
Nominee (OPC)
The person named by the single member of a One Person Company who would become the member if the owner dies or becomes incapable. The nominee must consent in writing at incorporation. Read more

O

OPC
A One Person Company, a company with a single shareholder and a nominee. It gives a solo founder limited liability and a separate legal identity with company-style compliance. Read more
Operating cycle
The time between paying for materials and receiving cash from customers. It equals inventory days plus receivable days, less creditor days where relevant, and it drives working capital need. Read more
Overdraft (OD)
A facility that lets a current account go below zero up to a sanctioned limit, usually against security. Interest is charged on the amount overdrawn. Read more

P

PAN
The Permanent Account Number issued by the Income Tax Department. Individuals and entities need it for tax filings, opening bank accounts and registering for GST. Read more
Pitch deck
A short presentation, usually 10 to 15 slides, that introduces a business to investors: the problem, solution, market, traction, financials, team and the amount sought. Read more
Project report
A document describing a project or business: market, technical details, cost, means of finance and projected profits. Banks ask for it for new projects and term loans, often along with CMA data. Read more
Promoter's contribution
The part of a project's funding that the owners bring from their own resources. Banks usually require a minimum share so that promoters have their own money at stake. Read more

R

Registered valuer
A professional registered with the authority under company law to value assets such as shares and businesses. Certain valuations under the Companies Act and the insolvency code must be done by a registered valuer. Read more
ROC (Registrar of Companies)
The government office that registers companies, keeps their records and receives their annual filings. Each state has a registrar, and filings are made online. Read more

S

Sensitivity analysis
A test of how a result, such as a valuation or DSCR, changes when key assumptions change. It shows which assumptions matter most and the range of reasonable outcomes. Read more
Startup India recognition
A certificate from DPIIT confirming that an entity meets the definition of a startup, which allows access to benefits under the initiative, subject to the conditions of each scheme. Read more

T

TAN
The Tax Deduction and Collection Account Number, needed by anyone who deducts or collects tax at source. It must be quoted in TDS returns and certificates. Read more
TDS
Tax deducted at source: tax that the payer deducts from certain payments, such as salary or professional fees, and deposits with the government. Deductors file periodic returns. Read more
Term loan
A loan repaid in instalments over a fixed period, usually to finance machinery, buildings or expansion. Banks assess it using projections and the debt service coverage ratio. Read more
Terminal value
The value of a business beyond the explicit forecast period in a DCF. It is often estimated by assuming cash flows grow at a steady rate forever, and it can account for most of the total value. Read more
Trademark
A mark, name, logo or phrase that distinguishes the goods or services of one business from another. Registration gives the owner exclusive rights to use it for the registered goods and services. Read more

U

Udyam registration
The online registration of a micro, small or medium enterprise on the government's Udyam portal, based on a self-declaration linked to PAN and Aadhaar. It supports access to MSME benefits. Read more
Unit economics
The revenue and cost of a single unit of the business, such as one order or one customer. Investors use unit economics to see whether growth makes money or only adds losses. Read more

V

Valuation date
The date as at which a valuation is made. Values change over time, so a report is only reliable for the date it states and for uses that accept a report of that age. Read more

W

WACC
The weighted average cost of capital: the blended return required by a company's lenders and shareholders, weighted by how much each funds. It is a common starting point for the discount rate in a DCF. Read more
Working capital
The money tied up in running the business day to day, mainly inventory and receivables less creditors. Net working capital is current assets less current liabilities. Read more
