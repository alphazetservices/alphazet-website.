How We Prepare a CMA Report: Six Steps and the Checks Behind Them

A CMA report works when the numbers are consistent and the story behind them is believable. This is our process, step by step.

The six steps

1. Scope. We confirm the facility you need, the bank's format and the years to cover.

2. Collect. Financial statements, GST returns, bank statements, sanction letters, stock and debtor statements, and a note on the business.

3. Analyse history. We check margins, working capital cycles and debt, and note anything the bank will ask about.

4. Build projections. Sales, costs, working capital and capex, with the reason for each assumption written down.

5. Prepare the forms. Operating statement, balance sheet analysis, ratios, MPBF and funds flow in the bank's format.

6. Review and hand over. A second person checks the figures. You receive the report in Excel and PDF with a short note you can use when you meet the bank.

Data sources

The numbers come from your audited or provisional financial statements, your GST returns and bank statements for cross-checks, and your own projections discussed with us. We do not invent figures. Where information is missing, we ask for it or state the assumption clearly.

Ratio checks we run

We check the current ratio, the debt-to-net-worth ratio, the interest cover and the debt service coverage ratio across all years, and compare each with common bank norms, remembering that each bank sets its own. We also check that holding periods for inventory, receivables and creditors are consistent from year to year and match your real cycle.

Common reasons banks send reports back

Projections that do not follow from past figures. A balance sheet that does not balance. Different holding periods used in different years without an explanation. Missing notes on assumptions. Ratios outside the bank's norms with no comment. We review for each of these before the report leaves us.

What we do not do

We do not promise a sanction or a limit. The bank decides using its own criteria. Our job is to make your case complete, consistent and easy to read.
