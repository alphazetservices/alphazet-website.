Finance and Compliance Support for Manufacturing Businesses

A manufacturer carries raw material, work in progress and finished goods, and waits for customers to pay. That cycle ties up cash, which is why banks look closely at your working capital before they lend. We help manufacturing businesses present their numbers clearly and meet the paperwork that buyers and tenders ask for.

Problems we see in manufacturing businesses

Working capital cycles are long

Cash goes out for materials and wages weeks before it comes in from customers. If the bank sees the gap only on paper, it may size your limit too low. A clear projection of the cycle, with inventory, receivable and creditor days, shows the real need.

Inventory-heavy balance sheets

Large stock makes the current ratio look healthy while hiding slow-moving items. Banks ask for stock statements and may discount old stock. Keeping the stock figures consistent between your books and your bank statements avoids questions.

CC and OD limits that no longer fit

A limit set for last year's sales may not cover this year's growth. Asking for an enhancement needs fresh projections and a reason, in the format the bank uses.

Certifications and tenders

Larger buyers and government tenders often ask for quality certification and past turnover proof. Missing a document can end a bid before it begins.

What banks and investors look at

Banks reviewing a manufacturer usually check the sales trend, gross margin stability, the inventory and receivable days, the current ratio, the debt-to-net-worth ratio and the ability to repay term loans from profits. They compare your projections with your past figures and with how similar businesses in the sector operate. They are also alert to related-party dealings and to stock that is not moving. A report that explains each of these before they ask makes the conversation shorter.

How we work with you

We start with a short call to understand your products, customers and the way cash moves through your plant. Then we review your last two to three years of statements, test your projections against them and agree a written scope. For a bank proposal, we prepare CMA data in the bank's format with a short note you can use at the meeting. For growth plans, we build a model that links sales, capacity, working capital and loans so you can see what each decision does to cash. Everything is delivered in Excel and PDF, and we walk you through it.

Questions people ask

Which method does a bank use to set a working capital limit?

It depends on the bank and the size of the limit. Two common methods are the turnover method and the second method of lending. Our working capital calculator shows both.

What documents do I need for a CMA report?

Usually two to three years of financial statements, stock and debtor details, bank statements, existing sanction letters and your projections. Our CMA checklist lists them.

Can you help us enhance an existing CC limit?

Yes. We prepare the projections and the CMA data that support the request. The bank decides the limit.

Do we need ISO certification to supply to larger buyers?

Some buyers and tenders ask for it, others do not. Check the buyer's requirements first.
