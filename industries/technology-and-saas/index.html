Finance and Compliance Support for Technology and SaaS Businesses

Technology and SaaS founders raise money early, often before profits. Investors want clear numbers on growth, retention and cash runway, in a structure that supports share options for the team. We help with the valuation, the model, the deck and the registrations.

Problems we see in technology and saas businesses

Which valuation approach to use

Early-stage software is often discussed on revenue multiples, while investors who focus on cash flow prefer a discounted cash flow. Each has a place. We show both and explain where they differ.

Investors want unit economics

Customer acquisition cost, lifetime value, churn and payback period decide how fast you can grow. A financial model that connects these to the profit and cash flow answers the first questions an investor will ask.

Getting ready for ESOPs and equity funding

A Private Limited company can issue shares and options. An LLP cannot. Choosing the right structure early avoids a costly conversion before a funding round.

Startup recognition and benefits

DPIIT recognition may give access to certain benefits. Eligibility is set by the government, and we help you prepare the application.

What banks and investors look at

Investors in software businesses look at recurring revenue, growth rate, gross margin, churn, customer concentration and the cash runway. They check whether your projections follow from your history and whether your assumptions about hiring and marketing spend are realistic. They also read your cap table and your shareholder agreements. Having these consistent across the deck, the model and the valuation builds confidence.

How we work with you

We start with your metrics: customers, recurring revenue, churn and cash runway. We build a model that connects these to the profit and loss and cash flow, then use it as the base for the valuation and the pitch deck, so every number agrees. We explain the valuation in the way an investor will read it, show a sensitivity table and flag the assumptions that need the strongest evidence. If you need to register a company, apply for DPIIT recognition or set up monthly CFO reporting, we handle those too.

Questions people ask

Should a SaaS startup use DCF or revenue multiples?

Often both. Multiples reflect what the market pays for similar companies, and DCF reflects your own projected cash flow. A valuation can reconcile the two.

Do I need a registered valuer for fundraising?

Not usually for a private negotiation. A registered valuer's report may be required for certain regulatory purposes, such as some share allotments.

Can you build the financial model and the deck together?

Yes. Building them together keeps the numbers the same everywhere.

What does a virtual CFO do for a startup?

Tracks cash and burn, prepares monthly reports and supports fundraising. The scope is agreed in writing.
