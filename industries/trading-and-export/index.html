Finance and Compliance Support for Trading and Export Businesses

Traders and exporters live on thin margins and long receivable cycles. A delayed payment from one buyer can strain the whole business. We help trading and export businesses keep their books clean, file GST on time and present a working capital case that reflects how they really earn.

Problems we see in trading and export businesses

Receivables drive the working capital need

When your buyers take 60 to 90 days to pay, the funding gap is mostly receivables. Banks ask for a debtor list with ageing, and they look for concentration in a few buyers.

Thin margins leave little room for error

A small mistake in costing, freight or foreign exchange can erase the profit on a shipment. Good bookkeeping that tracks each trade helps you see which lines actually pay.

GST on goods that cross state lines

Supplying goods to another state generally requires GST registration, and exports have their own treatment and refund routes. Timely filing and correct classification protect your input tax credit.

Export documentation

Exporters need an Importer Exporter Code and consistent shipping, invoice and bank documents. A mismatch between them can delay payment or refunds. We help with the financial and tax side; shipping and customs agents handle the physical documentation.

What banks and investors look at

Lenders to traders and exporters look at the quality of receivables, the spread of buyers, the margin on each product line, the speed of the stock cycle and the consistency between your GST returns, your books and your bank statements. For exporters they also look at the payment terms and the currencies you are paid in. Making these visible in the report reduces back-and-forth.

How we work with you

We begin by understanding your product lines, buyers and credit terms. We then clean up the records that feed your reports: sales and purchase registers, GST returns and bank statements. Once the books agree with each other, we prepare the CMA data or the monthly reports you need. For exporters, we add the currency and payment terms to the projections, so the bank sees the real cycle. We keep GST filing on a calendar so returns go in before the due date, and we flag mismatches while they are still easy to fix.

Keep these ready

  • Sales and purchase registers
  • GST returns for the last 12 months
  • Debtor list with ageing by buyer
  • Bank statements for 12 months
  • IEC and export documents, if you export
  • Last two to three years of financial statements

Questions people ask

Do I need GST registration to export?

Many exporters register because they need to claim refunds and supply to other states. Use our GST checker for your situation and confirm the details with us.

What is an IEC?

The Importer Exporter Code is issued by the Directorate General of Foreign Trade and is needed for most import and export activity. We can advise on the financial side and point you to the right process.

Can bookkeeping reduce my GST errors?

Yes. Matching sales and purchase records every month makes returns faster and catches mismatches early.

How do banks view a business with a few large buyers?

Concentration is a risk they ask about. Showing the history of payments from those buyers helps.
