CMA Report for a Bank Loan: Format, Documents and Common Mistakes

How to prepare a CMA report for a bank loan: the format, documents, how to set projections and the mistakes that make banks send reports back.

What the bank wants from your report

When you apply for a working capital limit or a term loan, the credit officer has to answer three questions: how much does this business need, how will it use the money and how will it repay. A CMA report answers all three in a format the bank can compare with other borrowers.

A strong report is not a longer report. It is one where every number agrees with every other number, every assumption has a reason and the story the figures tell matches what the officer sees in your bank statements and GST returns. This guide walks through how to prepare one.

Start with the right facility

Decide what you are asking for before you build numbers. A cash credit or overdraft limit finances day-to-day working capital and moves up and down with use. A term loan finances assets such as machinery and is repaid in instalments. Some businesses need both, and the report must show each separately.

Ask your bank for its CMA format and the number of years it wants. Banks usually want two to three years of past results, an estimate for the current year and projections for the next two to five years, with term loans needing projections for the full repayment period.

Gather the documents first

Collect documents before you build projections, because they decide what you can credibly project.

  • Audited or provisional financial statements for the last two to three years, with schedules.
  • The current year's results to date and a stock statement.
  • Debtor and creditor lists with ageing.
  • Bank statements for 12 months and sanction letters for existing loans.
  • GST returns and income tax returns, so figures can be cross-checked.
  • Quotations for machinery or capital items if you are asking for a term loan.

Build projections that follow from your history

Start from last year's actual results and move forward with stated reasons. If you project sales to grow 30 per cent while history shows 8 per cent, write down what changes: a new order, a new plant, a new market. If margins improve, say why: lower material cost, better pricing, scale.

Keep working capital days consistent with your real cycle. If your debtors take 60 days to pay today, projecting 30 days needs a reason. Banks notice when holding periods change from year to year without explanation.

The forms the bank reads

The CMA format has six forms covering existing and proposed limits, the operating statement, the balance sheet analysis, the comparison of current assets and liabilities, the MPBF calculation and the fund flow. You can see a worked example in our sample CMA report, which uses a fictional company.

Each form depends on the one before it, so an error in the operating statement flows through to the ratios and the MPBF. That is why the best way to prepare a CMA is in one linked Excel model, not as six separate tables.

Check the ratios before the bank does

Calculate the current ratio, the debt-to-net-worth ratio, the interest coverage ratio and the debt service coverage ratio for every year. Compare them with the norms your bank is likely to use. If a ratio falls outside the norm in a projected year, either change the plan or add a note that explains it. Our DSCR calculator helps you check repayment cover year by year.

Common mistakes that cause a report to be returned

  • Projections that do not follow from the past with no explanation.
  • A balance sheet that does not balance, or totals that do not match schedules.
  • Different holding periods used in different years.
  • Figures that do not match GST returns or bank statements.
  • Missing notes on assumptions.
  • Asking for a limit that is far above the MPBF without supporting reasons.
  • Forgetting existing loans, guarantees or related-party balances.

What happens after you submit

The bank reviews the data, may ask questions, may visit your premises and prepares a credit note. It may sanction the limit you asked for, a lower limit or different terms. If the bank sends the report back, read the query carefully, correct the numbers and resubmit with a note explaining the change.

Questions people ask

How many years of data does a bank want?

Usually two to three years of past results, the current year's estimate and projections for the next two to five years. Check your bank's format.

Can I prepare a CMA myself?

Yes, if you know the format and your numbers are clean. Many businesses get help because errors cause delays.

How long does it take to prepare?

It depends on how ready your records are. With complete documents, the first draft can be ready in several working days.

What if the bank sanctions less than I asked for?

You can discuss the reasons, adjust the plan or provide more evidence. The decision stays with the bank.

Do I need audited accounts?

Banks usually prefer audited accounts for past years. Provisional figures may be accepted for the current year.
