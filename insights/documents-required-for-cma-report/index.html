Documents Required for a CMA Report: Complete Checklist

The documents a CMA report needs for a bank loan: financial statements, stock and debtor lists, loan details and what to check before you hand them over.

By Harivadan Dabhi · Published 16 November 2026 · Updated Invalid Date · 4 min read

  2. Financial statements
  3. Working capital documents
  4. Loan and project details

Why the document list matters

A CMA report is only as reliable as the papers behind it. The data in it must agree with your balance sheet, your stock and debtor records and your bank statements, because the credit officer will compare them. Most delays happen not in preparing the report but in finding documents that were never filed or that do not agree.

This checklist covers what most banks and CMA preparers ask for. Your bank may ask for more or in a different form, so confirm with your branch first. For the format and the steps, read our guide to the CMA report for a bank loan.

Financial statements

The core of the report is your past performance. Collect these in finalised form wherever possible.

If your latest year is not yet audited, bring a provisional balance sheet and profit and loss account and say clearly that they are provisional. Banks accept this, but they will ask for the audited figures when they are ready.

  • Audited or finalised balance sheet and profit and loss account for the last two to three years, with schedules and notes
  • Provisional or unaudited figures for the current year to date
  • Auditor's report and tax audit report, where applicable
  • Computation of income and income tax returns with acknowledgements for the same years
  • Trial balance or ledger extracts for any figure that needs explaining

Working capital documents

For a working capital limit, the bank needs to see how much stock you hold, how much your customers owe and how much you owe your suppliers. These drive the assessment of your limit, so the lists must be current and tie to the balance sheet.

  • Stock statement with the latest month-end position, split into raw material, work in progress and finished goods where relevant
  • Debtor list with ageing, showing amounts outstanding by customer and period
  • Creditor list with ageing
  • Details of existing bank limits: bank, type of facility, sanctioned limit, outstanding and security given
  • Bank statements for all accounts for the last 12 months

Loan and project details

The bank will ask what the money is for and how you will repay it. A clear purpose and a realistic plan make the credit officer's job easier and shorten the discussion.

  • Amount and type of facility you are asking for, such as cash credit, overdraft or term loan
  • Purpose of the loan and, for a term loan, quotations for machinery or the project cost
  • Your growth assumptions for the next two to three years, with the reasons behind them
  • Details of existing loans, repayment schedules and any loans from directors or relatives
  • A short note on your business, customers, suppliers and main products

Statutory and identity documents

Banks also need to verify who you are and that you are compliant. Keep these ready alongside the financial papers.

  • PAN of the business and of the proprietors, partners or directors
  • Incorporation certificate, partnership deed or LLP agreement, with the memorandum and articles for a company
  • GST registration and the latest GST returns
  • Udyam registration certificate, if you are a micro or small enterprise
  • KYC of proprietors, partners or directors and address proof of the business place
  • Details of property offered as security, with title documents where the bank needs them

Check these before you submit

A few checks save weeks. Make sure the sales in your GST returns are close to the sales in your accounts, and be ready to explain any gap. Confirm that stock and debtor figures match the balance sheet. Check that the loan you list matches the lender's statement, and that the names and addresses are the same on every document.

If your books are behind, finish them first. A CMA built on incomplete books will have to be redone when the numbers change.

What happens after you hand them over

We review the documents, list what is missing and agree the scope in writing. We then prepare the CMA data, the projections and the ratios, and walk you through them before you submit to the bank. The decision on the loan always rests with the bank.

Questions people ask

Is a CMA report possible without audited accounts?

Many banks accept provisional figures for the current year but expect audited statements for earlier years. Ask your branch what it needs.

How many years of financials does a CMA need?

Banks typically ask for the last two to three years, the current year estimate and projections, but each bank sets its own rule.

Do I need a project report as well?

For a new project or a term loan, the bank may ask for one. We explain the difference in our guide to project reports and CMA reports.

What if my GST sales and accounts sales differ?

Be ready to explain the gap, for example timing, advance receipts or credit notes. Unexplained gaps lead to queries.
