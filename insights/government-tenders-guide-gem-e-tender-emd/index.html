Government Tenders in India: GeM, e-Tender and EMD Guide

How to bid for government tenders in India: where they are published, eligibility, GeM, nProcure, EMD and MSME exemptions, and how to submit a clean bid.

By Harivadan Dabhi · Published 16 November 2026 · Updated Invalid Date · 3 min read

What this guide covers

Government buyers purchase goods, services and works through published tenders and through the Government e-Marketplace. This guide explains where to find them, what eligibility means, how EMD works and how to submit a bid that is not rejected on paper.

Each tender has its own conditions, and the notice always prevails over general information such as this.

Where tenders are published

Central departments and public sector units publish on the Central Public Procurement Portal and on their own portals. Gujarat state departments, boards and municipal bodies publish many tenders on nProcure. The Government e-Marketplace (GeM) is used for a large share of purchases of standard goods and services. Each portal has its own registration and signature requirements.

Eligibility and documents

A bid is first checked against the eligibility conditions: turnover, experience in similar work, registrations and capacity. A bid that fails here is rejected before the price is opened. EMD, tender fee and MSME exemptions

Earnest Money Deposit (EMD) is a refundable amount that shows the bidder is serious, and the tender fee is a non-refundable charge for the document. Registered micro and small enterprises are exempt from both in many purchases under the public procurement policy for MSEs, with valid Udyam proof. The exact rule depends on the buyer and the notice, so read it each time. See MSME Udyam registration.

Submitting the bid

Upload early, check every file's name, size and format, confirm that the digital signature is valid, and save the acknowledgement. We do not promise that a bid will win. The buyer evaluates every bid on the notice.

Questions people ask

Where do I find Gujarat government tenders?

Many are published on nProcure. Central tenders are on the Central Public Procurement Portal, and many purchases happen on GeM.

Do I need a digital signature to bid?

Yes. e-tender portals normally require a Class 3 digital signature.

Can a new business win government work?

It can, if it meets the tender's conditions. Some tenders relax experience for startups and MSEs.
