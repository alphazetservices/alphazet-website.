Project Report vs CMA Report: Which One Does Your Bank Need?

The difference between a project report and a CMA report, what each contains, when banks ask for one or both and how to prepare them together.

Two documents, two jobs

Business owners often hear their bank ask for a project report in one conversation and a CMA report in the next, and wonder if they are the same. They are not. They answer different questions, and for some loans the bank needs both.

A project report describes a project or a business: what it is, why it will work, how much it will cost and how it will be funded. A CMA report presents the financial data in the bank's fixed format so that the credit officer can assess it against lending norms. This guide explains the difference.

What a project report contains

A project report, sometimes called a detailed project report or DPR, tells the story of the business or project in prose and numbers.

  • The promoters, their experience and the business background.
  • The product or service, the market and the competition.
  • Technical details, such as machinery, capacity, location and raw materials.
  • The cost of the project and the means of finance: promoter's contribution and loan.
  • Projected profit and loss, cash flow and break-even.
  • Repayment schedule and the key risks.

What a CMA report contains

A CMA report is mostly tables. It uses the bank's format to present your existing and proposed limits, your operating statement, your balance sheet analysis, your current assets and liabilities, the maximum permissible bank finance and your fund flow, over past, current and projected years. It carries few words beyond a short note on assumptions.

Which does your bank need?

Ask the bank for its checklist before you start, so you prepare only what it will read.

  • New business or a new project term loan: usually a project report, plus CMA data or a projected financial statement set.
  • Working capital limit for an existing business: usually CMA data and recent financial statements; a short business note may be enough.
  • Small loans and government schemes: a simpler project report in the scheme's format may be all that is required.
  • Large limits: both, with additional reports such as stock audits and inspections.

Prepare them together

The best way to avoid mismatches is to build one financial model and draw both documents from it. Assumptions, projections, ratios and the repayment schedule then stay consistent. A change in one assumption flows to every table.

If the bank asks for changes, you update the model once and regenerate both. Our financial modelling service builds this kind of linked model.

Common problems

  • The project report says one thing about sales and the CMA another.
  • The cost of the project differs between the two documents.
  • The repayment schedule in the project report does not match the loan terms in the CMA.
  • Technical details, such as capacity, do not support projected sales.
  • Promoters' contribution is not clearly shown.

A worked scenario

Whichever documents your bank requires, ask in advance for its checklist and its CMA format. A fifteen-minute call with the relationship manager can save weeks of rework.

Consider a manufacturer that wants to add a new production line and also needs a larger cash credit limit. The bank is likely to ask for a project report for the line, covering the machinery, its cost, the funding split, the market for the extra output and the repayment plan. It will also ask for CMA data for the enlarged business, which shows the combined operating statement, balance sheet and working capital need after the expansion.

If these are prepared separately, small differences creep in: the project report may assume the line starts in April and the CMA in June, or the project cost may differ by a few lakh. A single model avoids this, and it lets you answer the bank's questions with confidence because every figure traces back to the same assumption.

Questions people ask

Is a CMA report mandatory?

Not always. Banks ask for it for working capital limits above their internal threshold. Your bank can tell you.

Do I need both for a term loan?

Often a project report and projected financial data are needed. Whether the bank wants CMA forms depends on its policy.

Who signs the reports?

The promoter signs. Some banks also ask for a chartered accountant's certificate on the financial data.

Can the same consultant prepare both?

Yes, and it reduces the risk of mismatches.
