DCF Valuation Calculator for Startups and Companies

Turn five years of projected free cash flow into an enterprise and equity value.

Debt less cash. Use a negative number for net cash.

Enter your five years of free cash flow and a discount rate to see the valuation.

How this is calculated

  • Present value of year n = free cash flow(n) ÷ (1 + discount rate)^n.
  • Terminal value = free cash flow(year 5) × (1 + terminal growth) ÷ (discount rate − terminal growth), discounted back five years.
  • Enterprise value = sum of present values + present value of terminal value. Equity value = enterprise value − net debt.

About this tool

A discounted cash flow valuation estimates what a business is worth today from the cash it is expected to generate in future. You project the free cash flow for a number of years, discount each year back to today at a rate that reflects the risk, and add a terminal value for all the years beyond the projection.

This calculator uses five years of free cash flow and the Gordon growth method for the terminal value, which assumes cash flows grow at a steady rate after year five. Enter your projected figures, a discount rate and a terminal growth rate. The tool shows the present value of each year, the terminal value, the enterprise value and the equity value after net debt.

Two inputs drive most of the answer: the discount rate and the terminal growth rate. A small change in either moves the value a lot, so the sensitivity table shows equity value across a range of both. Look at the spread, not the single number. If the range is very wide, the valuation depends heavily on assumptions that need stronger evidence.

The terminal growth rate must be lower than the discount rate. Otherwise the formula gives a meaningless result, so the tool shows an error. Terminal growth is usually set close to long-term growth of the economy, not to the growth of a young business.

This calculator does not suggest a discount rate or any industry multiple, because the right rate depends on your business, its stage and its risks. A formal valuation report builds the discount rate step by step, tests the projections against your history and market, and may cross-check the result with a comparable-company method. For regulatory purposes, a registered valuer's report may be required.

Be careful with the cash flows you enter. Free cash flow means the cash left after operating costs, tax and the investment needed to grow, so a fast-growing business may have negative cash flow in early years, and that is fine to enter. What matters is that the projections are supported by your history, your pipeline and the market. Also check that net debt uses the same date as your valuation date. If you are valuing a startup with little history, treat the result as one input and use a second method to cross-check it.

Frequently asked questions

Which discount rate should I use?

It depends on the risk of the business. A valuation report builds it from a cost-of-capital approach. This tool does not suggest a rate.

What is terminal value?

The value of all cash flows after the projection period, estimated from the last year's cash flow and a steady growth rate.

Why must growth be below the discount rate?

If growth is equal or higher, the terminal value formula breaks down and gives zero or negative values.

Is this enough for share allotment or tax filings?

No. Regulatory valuations have their own requirements, and a registered valuer may need to sign.

Is my data stored?

No. The calculation runs in your browser unless you submit the optional form.
