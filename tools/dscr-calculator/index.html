How this is calculated

  • DSCR = (net profit after tax + depreciation + interest on term loan) ÷ (principal repayment + interest on term loan).
  • Average DSCR is the simple average of the yearly values with a repayment due.
  • The lowest DSCR and the year it occurs show your tightest year.

About this tool

The debt service coverage ratio, or DSCR, tells a lender whether your business earns enough cash from its operations to pay the loan instalments due in a year. A DSCR of 1 means the business earns exactly what it must pay. Anything lower means the repayment is not covered by that year's profits.

This calculator uses the common bank formula. The numerator is the cash available for repayment: net profit after tax, plus depreciation (a non-cash charge), plus interest on the term loan. The denominator is what you must pay in the year: the principal instalment plus that interest.

Banks look at DSCR for every year of the loan, and they look at the average and the lowest year. A healthy average can hide one weak year, usually the first year after the moratorium or a year with a large capital expenditure. That is why the tool shows the lowest value and the year it falls in, along with a chart.

Many lenders look for an average DSCR of roughly 1.25 to 1.5 or higher. This is an indicative range, not a rule. Each bank sets its own norm, and it varies by industry, loan type and your track record.

If your DSCR is low, there are usually three levers: a longer repayment period, a lower loan amount, or higher projected profit supported by evidence. Each has to be backed by realistic assumptions in your projections. A CMA report or a financial model shows the bank how you reach those numbers, which is what makes the DSCR believable.

To get the most from this tool, use the same projections you will give the bank. Enter profit after tax, not profit before tax, and take depreciation and interest from your projected statements, not from memory. If the loan has a moratorium, leave the moratorium years empty or enter only interest. Test a second case with a lower profit, because lenders often ask what happens if sales fall, and a DSCR that stays above one in a weaker case is easier to defend.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good DSCR?

Many lenders look for an average of roughly 1.25 to 1.5 or higher. Each bank sets its own norm, so confirm with your lender.

Why add back depreciation?

Depreciation reduces profit but is not a cash payment, so it is added back to find the cash available for repayment.

Which interest do I include?

Interest on the term loan being assessed. Working capital interest is normally treated separately.

What if I have no repayment in a year?

A year with no principal or interest due has no DSCR. Leave it empty and it will be skipped.

Is this a lender's decision?

No. It is an estimate to help you prepare. The bank decides using its own criteria.
