Private Limited vs LLP vs OPC: Business Structure Chooser

Eight questions, a suggested structure and the reasons behind it.

1. How many founders or owners will there be?
2. Do you plan to raise equity funding from investors?
3. Do you want to give employee stock options (ESOPs)?
4. What annual turnover do you expect in the first two years?
5. Will foreign investors or foreign owners be involved?
6. Do you want full control as a single owner?
7. How much compliance effort can you manage each year?
8. Do you need credibility with large clients or banks?

Answer all 8 questions to see the suggested structure.

How this is calculated

  • Each answer adds or removes points from each structure using a published rule table.
  • The structure with the highest total is suggested, and the answers that favoured it are listed as reasons.
  • The rules are indicative; they do not replace advice on your situation.

About this tool

Choosing between a Private Limited company, a Limited Liability Partnership and a One Person Company is one of the first decisions a founder makes. It affects who can own the business, whether you can raise equity funding, whether you can give ESOPs and how much compliance you take on every year.

A Private Limited company is the usual choice if you plan to raise equity from investors, give ESOPs, bring in foreign investment or deal with large clients and banks. It has the highest compliance load of the three, with ROC filings, audit and board meetings.

An LLP suits small teams that do not plan to issue shares. It has lighter annual compliance and flexible internal rules, but it cannot issue shares or ESOPs, and most equity investors prefer a company.

A One Person Company is meant for a single owner who wants a separate legal entity with limited liability. It needs a nominee and has its own rules on conversion as the business grows, so check the current rules before choosing it.

This tool asks eight questions and scores each structure from your answers. It then shows the structure that scores highest and the specific answers that favoured it. If the options are close, the tool says so. The result is a starting point for a conversation, not a recommendation: tax, sector, location and the founders' plans can change the answer. Our registration team can walk you through the trade-offs before you file.

Think about where the business will be in three years, not only today. A structure that is cheapest now may be expensive to change later, and investors, banks and large customers often have a preference. Also consider who will sign what: directors and partners have different duties and liabilities. If your answers are mixed, with some favouring a company and some an LLP, ask yourself which factor is hardest to change later, and weigh that one most.

Frequently asked questions

Can an LLP raise venture capital?

An LLP cannot issue shares, and most equity investors prefer companies. Many startups choose a Private Limited company for this reason.

Can I convert later?

Conversions between structures are possible, but they take time and cost. It is cheaper to choose well at the start.

Which has the lowest compliance?

An LLP generally has lighter annual filings than a company.

Who can form an OPC?

A single person who is a resident individual, with a nominee named at incorporation. Check the current eligibility rules before applying.

Is this legal advice?

No. It is indicative guidance to help you prepare questions for a professional.
