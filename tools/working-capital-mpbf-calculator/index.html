Working Capital and MPBF Calculator for Bank Loans

See how much working capital finance a bank may consider, by two standard methods.

Creditors, advances received, provisions, statutory dues.

Enter your projected turnover to see the result. Current assets are needed for the second method.

How this is calculated

  • Turnover method: working capital need = 25% of projected turnover; bank finance up to 20% of turnover; promoter margin 5% of turnover.
  • Second method of lending: MPBF = 75% of current assets less other current liabilities. The remaining 25% of current assets is the margin you fund from long-term sources.
  • Projected current ratio = current assets ÷ (other current liabilities + bank finance).

About this tool

When a business applies for a cash credit or overdraft limit, the bank first estimates how much working capital the business needs and how much of it the bank can finance. Two methods are in everyday use. This calculator shows both side by side so you can see the range before you speak to a banker.

The turnover method is the simpler one. It takes your projected annual turnover and assumes the working capital need is a quarter of it. The bank finances up to a fifth of turnover and the promoter brings the remaining five per cent as margin. It is generally used for smaller limits, and your bank's own policy sets the exact cut-off.

The second method of lending looks at your balance sheet instead. It takes your projected current assets (inventory, receivables, cash and other current assets), subtracts your current liabilities other than the bank loan, and lets the bank finance that gap after keeping a quarter of current assets as your own margin. The result is called the maximum permissible bank finance, or MPBF.

Both methods are only a starting point. Banks look at your past financials, the quality of receivables, inventory ageing, the industry and your relationship with them before they sanction a limit. They may also use a different method for a limit of your size. A CMA report puts these numbers into the format banks expect, with projected operating statements, balance sheets, fund flow and ratios, so the limit you ask for is supported by the data.

Use the result to prepare, not to promise. If the two methods give very different numbers, it usually means your current assets are large compared with your turnover, or your other current liabilities are small. Both are things a CMA report can explain to the bank.

Keep your inputs realistic. Banks compare your projected turnover with your past turnover and with your GST returns, so a projection far above your history needs an explanation. Use inventory and receivable levels that match your real operating cycle, and include only genuine current liabilities. If you run the calculator for two scenarios, such as a cautious and a growth case, you will see how sensitive the limit is to your assumptions, which helps you decide what to ask the bank for.

Frequently asked questions

What is MPBF?

MPBF stands for maximum permissible bank finance. Under the second method of lending it is 75% of current assets less other current liabilities.

Which method will my bank use?

That depends on the bank's policy and the size of the limit you ask for. Ask your relationship manager, and treat these results as an estimate.

Is the turnover method always 20%?

20% bank finance with 5% promoter margin is the commonly quoted figure for the turnover method. Your bank may apply its own policy.

What goes under other current liabilities?

Creditors, advances from customers, provisions and statutory dues. Exclude the bank borrowing you are applying for.

Is my data stored?

No. The calculation runs in your browser. Nothing is sent unless you choose to submit the optional form.
